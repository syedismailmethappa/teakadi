Our craft

Tea, taken seriously
but never stiffly

Kōcha & Co. started as a two-burner cart outside a rope factory. Seven years on we have a room, a granite mill and a stubborn rule: if we can't trace the leaf, we don't pour it.

The room

Built for slow afternoons

Dark timber, warm lamps and a counter you can sit at with your elbows on it. The bar faces the mill so you can watch your matcha being ground — it takes a while, and that's the point.

Seven years of tuning

One recipe book, hundreds of revisions. Every tea has a steep card behind the bar.

Nine farming families

Long-term contracts and above-market prices. We buy the harvest before we taste it.

Process

What happens before you sip

Two harvests a year, four hands on the leaf, one very patient mill.

Cupping table

Every lot is brewed blind at three temperatures. Only lots that hold up twice make the board.

Rest & roast

Oolongs rest six weeks; our charcoal roast is done in small 2kg batches over binchōtan.

Mill to order

Matcha is ground the morning it's served. Oxygen is the enemy of that bright jade green.

Pour & serve

Weighed leaves, measured water, a timer. Then we hand it over and get out of your way.

Low waste

Nothing steeped twice goes in the bin

Spent leaves are dried and ground into our pastry flour, and returned cups earn you $0.30 off the next brew.

Returnable cups

Bring the cup back, get a discount. Over 40% of hot drinks go out in a returnable.

Second-life leaves

Spent hojicha and genmaicha become the base of our cookie and cheesecake crumb.

Behind the counter

Three people, one obsession

Mina Arakawa — Founder

Started the cart. Still mills every batch of ceremonial matcha herself, at 7am.

Dev Chatterjee — Tea Lead

Runs the cupping table and the charcoal roaster. Will talk about oxidation for hours.

Rosa Lindqvist — Pastry

Turns our spent leaves into the pastry case. The Basque cheesecake is her fault.
